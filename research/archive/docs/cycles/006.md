## 1. What the audit contradicted in the previous briefings

**The gap is one sentence: cycle 005 pointed at exactly the right place to
look, three times, and was right each time — but the thing found there was
adjacent to the thing feared, and in the one case it measured itself, it
counted the wrong population.**

**1 — The number that closes F-004-6 contains 18 counter-examples.** Cycle 005
§4 records *"1,072 documents with >1 chunk … C4 is satisfied by this count"*,
and is careful to say it was *"verified directly against the database, not
inferred from the log."* It was. The query was over all 2,585 documents. Of the
1,072: **18** are the baseline `policy`/`sop`/`sla_annexure`/`supplier_agreement`
documents, which had two chunks each *before this cycle began* — one table chunk
and one narrative chunk from `_extract_document_chunks`, not a `_chunk_words`
split — and which are precisely the corpus F-004-6's *"the chunker has never
chunked"* was written about. **741** are review digests under 512 words that
`_chunk_words` emitted whole and that only became multiple chunks when
`_embed_allowing_split` rescued them. **313** — cycle 005's own pre-ingest
dry-run figure, quoted in its DEVIATION 2 — are the real `_chunk_words` splits.
The claim is true (313 > 0); the number published as its measure is 3.4× it.

**The mechanism is the transferable part, and it is a §7 gap.** Criterion C4
read *"at least one ingested document produces more than one chunk, and the
outcome records how many documents did."* When it was frozen,
`_embed_allowing_split` **did not exist**, so that phrase could only mean
`_chunk_words`. The fallback was added mid-build — correctly, in response to a
real failure, and disclosed — and it silently changed what the criterion
measured **without changing a word of it**. §4.4 protects criteria text from
moving. Nothing protects against the world moving underneath the text.

**2 — Cycle 005 §5.1 named the query it should have run. I ran it, and both
halves of the answer matter.** Its worry was that the "separate regions of
embedding space" reading of the M6 null was interpretation, not test. That
reading **survives**: review chunks are genuinely retrievable and take all
eight top slots for a review-topical query, so the null is not the artefact of
an unretrievable corpus. What the same probe also shows is that the corpus is
**not addressable** — a query naming a seller's exact id ranks that seller's own
digest **2,365 of 13,161**, against `semantic_top_k=8`, while returning eight
other sellers. 10,558 of 13,125 chunks (80.4%) contain no occurrence of their
own seller id.

**3 — Cycle 005 §5.3 feared the fallback's boundaries might be bad. They are,
and its own estimate of the blast radius is low by 5×.** §5.3 says the unchecked
boundary quality *"affects roughly 16% of the corpus's chunks (2,119 of
13,125)"*. 2,119 is the count of *input* chunks that needed splitting; the chunks
they produced are the output. **11,612 of 13,125 chunks (88.5%) sit in documents
that were split**, no chunk in the corpus exceeds 154 words against a 512-word
target, and **94.9% of continuation chunks begin mid-review** — some mid-
identifier, cut through the middle of an order id.

**4 — Cycle 001 §5.2 sent a future cycle to `oh_j04` first. It was right, for a
different reason than it gave.** Its worry was multi-payment-type attribution
semantics; that reading holds up. What is wrong with `oh_j04` is that it is
labelled `join3` and is **answerable with a two-table join** — `orders` is a
pure pass-through, and both graded models dropped it and scored correct. Cycle
002's safeguard 6 cannot see this: it checks the *ground truth's* table count,
not whether the *question* requires one.

**What is NOT contradicted, and is worth saying plainly.** Cycle 005's
determinism claim reproduces byte-for-byte today. Its reversibility
demonstration, its A5-fingerprint check, its tenant/`doc_type` DEVIATION-1
reasoning, its security-header fix and its live-response check all hold. All
six gates reproduce at HEAD. The M6 figures are untouched and, so far as this
audit tested, untouchable by the ingest. Cycle 005 did careful work and
disclosed its gaps accurately enough that this audit was mostly a matter of
going where it pointed.

## 2. The plan

**Shape: correct the record, then make the corpus do the one thing a per-seller
digest exists to do.** Two of the six findings are about published numbers not
matching the artifact; those are cheap and go first. One is about the artifact
itself being unusable for its purpose; that is the cycle's engineering work. Two
are disclosure items in the shape cycle 005's B1–B3 already established. One is
an owner call and is not mine to make.

### Measurement discipline

Every arm runs with shipped flags via `shipped_flags`, or records its deviation.
No fixture, rubric, scorer or committed report JSON is edited; reports go to new
`--out` paths. The re-ingest changes the corpus M6 is measured over, so M6 is
measured **after** it and compared against cycle 005's committed after-arm
(`docs/bench_grounding_report_005_m2_after.json`) as the before.

  generator assigned by construction, with no judgement in it.
- **No entity extraction, no query-side seller resolution, no hybrid lexical
  retrieval.** Each is a new feature (§3 SETTLED) and B is deliberately scoped
  to the ingestion side of the same seam.

## 3. DEVIATIONS

*(appended during the build, as they occur — §5.2)*

**DEVIATION 1 — A3's pinning test caught a second error inside my own A1/A2
first-pass correction, before either was committed; the real split is
349/705, not 313/741.**

Building A3 (a test that calls `_chunk_words` directly against the manifest,
because that's the function the claim is about) produced 349, not the 313 I
had just written into A1/A2 from re-running the generator's own diagnostic
print. The diagnostic used `content_md.count(" ") + 1` as a word-count proxy;
`_chunk_words` tokenises with `str.split()`, which also treats a newline as a
word boundary, and digest text is newline-heavy — so the proxy undercounts.
Cross-checked against the live database (independent of both the generator
and this test): the real ingest used the actual `_chunk_words`, so the
database's persisted chunk counts confirm 349 is exact — `real_split ⊆
db_multi`, and the remainder (705) is fallback-only.

Not a moved criterion — A1/A2/A3's text is unchanged; the number that fills
it in changed twice before it was ever committed. Nothing with 313/741 was
ever pushed or shared, so I corrected in place rather than commit-then-revert.
Recorded as a DEVIATION anyway because G7 asks for exactly this shape of
event to be written down (a criterion's *meaning* — here, "the count `_chunk_
words` alone produces" — moving because of something discovered mid-build),
and because it is my own regression, reported per the binding rule that
applies to self-caught errors the same as any other. Root-caused at the
source: `scripts/gen_olist_review_digests.py`'s diagnostic now tokenises the
same way `_chunk_words` does. Full transcript: `docs/audits/006.md` F-006-1's
second amendment (F-006-1b). Commits: `4b5deb6`, `ff17145`.

**DEVIATION 2 — B4's re-ingest needed a real, unplanned performance fix to
`ingest.py` before it could complete in a usable timeframe; the criterion's
text (B4: "re-ingest through the existing `scripts/ingest.py` path") did not
anticipate this, and the fix changes what "the existing path" means for
review-digest documents specifically.**

**What happened, in order.** B3's section-based redesign produces 20,209
embedding calls (vs. cycle 005's ~4,686 for the whole-blob approach). The
first real attempt at B4, using the existing single-call `_embed`/`_embed_
allowing_split` path unchanged, was killed by the owner's own observation
("been almost 4 hours") after running that long with no end in sight —
investigated live: the embedding server genuinely was still working (`pg_
stat_activity` showed a real INSERT 0.7s before the check, `ollama`'s
`llama-server` process had accumulated real, growing CPU time across ~10
threads), not stuck, but the true per-call rate under sustained load
(measured directly: ~1.38s/call) made the full run ~7.8 hours — an order of
magnitude past what a reasonable ingest should cost, and past what cycle
005's comparable job took (~2 hours, for 4.3x fewer calls).

**Terminated cleanly, not force-killed blind.** `os.kill(pid, SIGTERM)`
against the process's PID as seen from *inside* the container's own PID
namespace (`docker top`'s PID is the host namespace's number and does not
match — found by checking `/proc/<pid>` from both sides before trusting
either). Verified immediately after: zero `statesync_ingest` connections in
`pg_stat_activity`, `document_chunks` back to its exact pre-attempt count
(2567/13125) — the transaction rolled back cleanly, nothing partial was
left behind, matching `ingest()`'s own `with conn.transaction()` guarantee.

**Investigated rather than just parameter-tuned.** Ollama's `/api/embed`
(plural, batch) endpoint was tested and found ~43x faster than `/api/
embeddings` (singular) for the SAME text on a SYNTHETIC short test string —
but that number did not survive contact with real corpus content: a real
50-section batch of actual review text took ~41s, not ~1.6s, because the
batch endpoint's cost scales with total TOKEN VOLUME in the batch, not call
count, and my synthetic test used much shorter strings than real sections.
Measured honestly against real content: ~6.91ms/word, giving a real,
defensible estimate of ~1.8 hours for the full corpus (945,513 total words
across all sections) — in line with cycle 005's own ingest duration, not
an order of magnitude off it.

**A real correctness hazard was found and designed around, not assumed
away.** `/api/embed` does NOT reject an over-length input the way `/api/
embeddings` does (HTTP 500, "context length exceeded") — it SILENTLY
TRUNCATES. Verified directly: a 400-word, a 200-word, and a 5000-word
slice of the same source text sent through `/api/embed` returned
byte-identical embeddings (max component diff 0.0 between the 400- and
5000-word versions). This is invisible from the API response; nothing in
it distinguishes a correctly-embedded short text from a silently-truncated
long one. Consequence: the batch endpoint is used ONLY for sections
already independently verified, client-side, to be safely under the real
context length (`_SAFE_BATCH_WORD_LIMIT = 90`, itself measured against the
corpus's densest documents through the endpoint that DOES reject); anything
that somehow exceeds that bound is routed to the single-call, properly-
rejecting path instead (`_embed_one_review_section`, B3's existing
review-boundary-aware fallback), never to the batch path blind.

**Result: `scripts/ingest.py` gained `_batch_embed` and the `has_sections`
branch's two-tier routing (fast batch path for verified-safe sections, slow
rejecting path for anything that isn't) — a real code change beyond what
B3 specified, made necessary by B4's own timing, not by a new requirement.**
The real, full re-ingest then completed cleanly: `Ingestion complete
(transaction committed)`, `2567 documents, 20209 chunks, 0 is_table (0.0%),
0 context-length split events` — every section embedded successfully on
the first try, confirming the packer's 60-word budget holds across the
ENTIRE corpus, not just the tested samples. A5 fingerprint unchanged before
and after (`cf4caa5c…`, byte-identical). B3's two properties verified
directly against the database, at 100%: 20209/20209 chunks contain their
own seller id, 0/20209 begin mid-review. Baseline corpus (36 chunks)
untouched. Reversibility re-demonstrated live: `DELETE` removed exactly
20,209 chunks / 2,567 documents, verified back to exactly 18/36, then
restored by re-running the same ingest.

**Not asked before acting.** This is not a reversal of any §4.3 owner
decision, and stopping a multi-hour job that was not going to finish in a
reasonable time to investigate and fix the actual bottleneck is the
conservative choice, not the risky one — flagged in full here per §5.2,
after being reported to the owner in the moment it happened.

## 4. Outcome

*(written during and after the build, as results land — §5.1)*

### B2/B5 — probe rank, before vs. after

0/20 probes rank within `semantic_top_k=8` in either arm — the headline
addressability bar is not cleared. Median rank improved (597 → 298, before
`docs/review_probe_rank_before.json` / after `docs/review_probe_rank_after.json`),
but the aggregate hides a real, mechanistically explained split: **11/11
low-review-count probes (≤5 reviews) got WORSE; 7/9 high-review-count probes
(>5 reviews) got BETTER.** Every low-review-count seller was already a
single chunk before B3 (nothing about their own representation changed);
every one of them saw their relative rank fall purely because the
retrieval space grew (13,161 → 20,245 chunks, +54%) with no compensating
signal of their own. Every high-review-count seller previously had most of
their chunks carrying NO seller-id text at all (F-006-2's 80.4% finding) —
after B3, ALL of their chunks carry it, and for several this pulls their
best chunk's rank up by 900–1,650 positions. This is exactly what was
told to the owner before this scope was chosen (*"I expect the attribution
fix to land cleanly and the retrieval-rank fix to be partial at best"*) —
confirmed, not merely asserted. **B3's actual properties (which is what it
was scoped to fix) are separately verified at 100%** against the database,
independent of this rank measurement: 20,209/20,209 chunks carry their own
seller id, 0/20,209 begin mid-review.

### B6 — M6 re-run: real movement, investigated, and NOT attributable to the review corpus

Cycle 005 measured this exact before/after pair as a clean null across
every field. This cycle's after-arm (`docs/bench_grounding_report_006_after.json`,
vs. cycle 005's committed `docs/bench_grounding_report_005_m2_after.json`)
is **not** a null:

| | cycle 005 after | cycle 006 after |
|---|---|---|
| mean_context_precision | 0.6222 | 0.6444 |
| mean_context_recall | 0.80 | 0.8333 |
| mean_faithfulness (documents only) | 0.8958 | 0.8542 |
| mean_faithfulness (+ SQL) | 0.875 | 0.96875 |
| total_ungrounded_but_true (all-sources) | 3 | 0 |

Per the criterion's own condition ("any movement at all is investigated and
explained... an unexplained change would mean something else moved too"),
here is the investigation, not just the numbers.

**14 of 16 queries' cited chunk lists are byte-identical to cycle 005's.**
Two differ: `e1_hybrid_001` and `e1_hybrid_005`. **Zero review chunks
appear in either query's cited list, before or after** — both diffs are
entirely among pre-existing baseline `supplier_agreement`/`sla_annexure`
documents, untouched by this cycle's ingest (verified: baseline chunk
counts unchanged, 36 total, exact same four doc_types/counts as before B4).
So the movement is not the review corpus intruding on retrieval.

**The actual cause: `document_chunks.embedding` is indexed HNSW
(`pg_indexes`: `USING hnsw (embedding vector_cosine_ops)`), an approximate
nearest-neighbor structure, not exact search.** Bulk-inserting 20,209 new
vectors into that same index changes its internal graph, which can shift
which candidates an approximate search surfaces for a near-tied query —
independent of whether the new vectors are topically related to the
query at all. Verified this is graph structure, not query-time
randomness: the same query against the same (current) index returns
byte-identical results on repeat.

**Confirmed against exact (brute-force) scores, computed by disabling the
index (`SET enable_indexscan=off; SET enable_bitmapscan=off`) and scoring
directly.** For `e1_hybrid_005` ("How many fulfillments has FastShip 1
handled, and what transit time does FastShip 1 commit to for the Metro
Mumbai zone?", expected chunk `doc_sla_annexure_LP001_c000`): exact scores
are `LP007_c001 0.7532 > LP001_c000 0.7113 > LP007_c000 0.6693 >
LP003_c000 0.6454`. Cycle 005's approximate index returned `[LP007_c000,
LP007_c001, LP003_c000]` — the WRONG 3rd item; the true rank-2 item,
`LP001_c000` (the expected answer), was never surfaced. This cycle's index
returns `[LP007_c000, LP007_c001, LP001_c000]` — the correct top-3. This
is why `e1_hybrid_005`'s own precision/recall moved from **0.0/0.0** to
**0.333/0.5** — a real per-query improvement, and it was ALWAYS latent: the
approximate index was silently wrong in cycle 005's committed report, and
an unrelated bulk insert happened to correct it.

**Not a uniformly benign story — checked the other direction too.** For
`e1_hybrid_001` (unscored: `expected_chunk_ids: []`, "checked directly by
E1-V4, not scored here"), exact scores are a genuine near-tie: `S001_c000
0.7143 ≈ S005_c000 0.7137 ≈ S025_c000 0.7134` (within 0.001 of each
other), with `S005_c001` clearly worse at `0.5678`. Cycle 005's index
returned the correct near-tied trio (`S005_c000, S001_c000, S025_c000`);
this cycle's returned `S005_c001` — the ONE candidate that exact scoring
says is clearly wrong — in place of `S025_c000`. **This query's own
`faithfulness_score` was unaffected (0.5 both times)** because it carries
no scored ground truth, so it does not move any published number — but it
is a real degradation in what the index actually returned, in the opposite
direction from the query above, and is recorded here rather than only
reporting the direction that reads well.

**What this establishes, and what it does not.** It establishes that this
project's vector retrieval has been running on an approximate index the
whole time, that this index's answers are not stable under corpus-size
changes unrelated to a query's topic, and that this specific movement is
NOT the review corpus interfering with retrieval — the thing B6 existed to
rule out. It does **not** establish that HNSW's approximation error is
small in general, that the net direction (one query better, one worse,
neither the review corpus) generalizes beyond these two affected queries,
or that M6's headline figures are more trustworthy now than before — they
are DIFFERENT, by a mechanism now understood, not validated as more
correct. `docs/limitations.md` records this as a standing property of the
system (§ below), not just an explanation for this cycle's number.

## 5. Confidence — where I would look if I were auditing me

**5.1 — The two-tier batch/fallback safety design in `ingest.py` was never
exercised on its unsafe branch, in production.** `_SAFE_BATCH_WORD_LIMIT=90`
exists specifically to route anything over that threshold to the
single-call, properly-rejecting `_embed_one_review_section` path instead of
the silently-truncating batch one. Across the entire real corpus, the
largest section built by the packer is 62 words — meaning `unsafe_idx` was
empty for every one of 2,567 documents, both real ingest runs. My only
evidence that the routing logic itself is correct is a synthetic stress
test I built by hand (an artificially oversized 381-word section, recursed
into 5 pieces, every one verified starting at a review boundary). If a
future ingest ever produces a section over 90 words — a different corpus,
a changed `SECTION_WORD_BUDGET`, a bug — this is the path that would
silently fail if it is wrong, and nothing in this cycle proved it right
under real conditions.

**5.2 — I found the mechanism behind two moved M6 queries; I did not check
whether the other fourteen are equally fragile.** The HNSW investigation
(§4, B6) explains *why* `e1_hybrid_001` and `e1_hybrid_005` moved and
confirms neither is review-content-driven. It does not establish that the
other fourteen queries' cited lists are robust — only that they didn't
move *this specific time*, on *this specific* 7,084-vector insert. A
near-tie that happened not to flip is not different in kind from one that
did; I did not compute exact-vs-approximate scores for the other fourteen
to see how close any of them sit to their own tie boundary.

**5.3 — The rank improvement's generalizability rests on one seed, one
question template, and a threshold I read off the data rather than
predicted.** `review_probe_set.json` is 20 sellers from one fixed seed,
each probed with exactly one phrasing ("What do customers say about seller
`<id>`?"). The clean 11-vs-9 review-count split is a real, verified pattern
in this sample — I did not generate a second, differently-seeded sample of
20 to see whether the same split holds, nor test a phrasing that doesn't
name the seller id verbatim (arguably the more realistic query shape — a
real user is unlikely to type a 32-hex id). The mechanism (chunks either
never had identity text or now all do) is sound and would be expected to
generalize; the specific numbers (11/11, 7/9) are one sample's worth of
confirmation, not a second, independent one.

**5.4 — Mistake 1's second-order nature is itself worth flagging: I have
not re-checked whether a THIRD proxy error exists.** `content_md.count("
") + 1` was wrong; I corrected it to `len(text.split())`, which is what
`_chunk_words` itself uses. I did not independently verify that `_chunk_
words`' own tokenisation is the "true" measure of what the embedding
model counts as a token — only that my diagnostic now agrees with the
function it's describing. Both could still be a coarse approximation of
the real BPE/subword tokenizer's count; I have empirical evidence this
approximation holds SAFELY (0/2,567 documents exceeded it) but not that
it's *exactly* right, only that it's conservative enough not to matter
here.

**5.5 — The owner's "been almost 4 hours" prompt is the reason mistake 2
was caught, not my own monitoring.** I had checked `pg_stat_activity` and
concluded the process was healthy roughly ninety minutes in and did not
re-examine my own time estimate after that — I was prepared to keep
waiting on an estimate I had not actually re-verified against elapsed
wall-clock time. If audited, I would want to know whether I would have
caught this on my own before the owner had to ask, and I do not think the
honest answer is yes.

**5.6 — `docs/audits/006.md`'s F-006-1 finding is now stale in a way I have
not gone back to annotate.** The audit (sealed, historical record per this
project's own convention) still states the 313/741 split as its finding,
with a second amendment correcting it to 349/705 appended afterward. A
reader who stops at the first amendment and doesn't reach the second would
carry the wrong number forward. This is the same convention cycle 004 and
005 used (amend, don't rewrite), and I believe it's the right one — but it
means the audit file alone, read partially, is not self-correcting the way
`limitations.md`'s single, current figure is.

**5.7 — I did not verify the D1/D2 topology fix by actually switching
`llm.ollama_url` and generating from the container.** The fix is a
documentation and comment correction based on reading the code and testing
the embedding path's model-equivalence; I did not exercise the "generate
from the Dockerized ollama" branch end-to-end to confirm the README's
corrected instructions actually work as written, only that they describe
the code accurately.

**5.8 — Every number in this build ran on `qwen2.5:7b` against the shipped
`public`/`olist` corpora; nothing here touches Gemini or any other
provider.** The rank measurements, the M6 re-run, and the batching
throughput figures are all specific to this local model and this
hardware's embedding server. None of it is a claim about how any other
provider or deployment would behave.
