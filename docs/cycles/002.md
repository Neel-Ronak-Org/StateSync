## 2. The plan

**Shape: correct the record, then remove the biggest confound under the
headline.**

Cycle 001 repaired the instrument and used it once. It produced a real headline
— `88.9% → 50.0%` — and published, alongside it, a defect-rate statistic that
is wrong by 4×, a failure analysis that is accurate for 3 of 8 cases, and a
credit to an automated check that never fired. None of those move the accuracy
numbers. All of them are in the report, and one of them (F-002-5) points the
next piece of engineering at the wrong subsystem. **Workstream A corrects the
record.** It is mostly text, plus two small guards and one test.

**Workstream B is the reason this cycle is worth a model switch.** F-002-10
found that `olist` shows the SQL agent **no join graph at all** — no foreign
keys, no primary keys — while the synthetic corpus shows it seventeen edges.
Every question in the hard fixture but one is join-bearing. BD6 records 2-hop
joins as this system's measured weakness across four independent checks. The
two failures F-002-5 reclassifies as schema grounding are precisely a missing
edge. That is a large, unmeasured confound sitting directly under the number
this project is about to put in a report.

**It is an experiment, not a fix, and that distinction is load-bearing.** This
repo has measured two schema-context interventions **harmful** (`a4293e1`,
schema comments) and one **helpful** (`da6e49f`, projection discipline). The
prior on reasoning here is bad and the prior on measuring is good. So B commits
to a paired before/after and **no threshold in either direction** — both
outcomes are publishable:

- **If FKs help:** the published real-data numbers were depressed by a
  corpus-preparation asymmetry the synthetic baseline never had, and *"needs a
  schema with a declared join graph"* becomes a stated deployment precondition
  — a genuine limitation, since plenty of enterprise warehouses declare none.
- **If FKs do not help:** a plausible confound under `50.0%` is retired and the
  7B's join failures are model capability, which strengthens the current claim
  rather than weakening it.

**Why the migration and not a code change.** Adding constraints changes the
prompt through a path that already ships and is already proven (it renders 17
edges on `public` today), so the experiment isolates one variable with **zero
orchestrator code**. If it helps, *then* building a join-hints source for
warehouses that cannot add constraints is justified by evidence. That is §5a's
own sequencing — watch it break, then implement against the observed failure —
applied one level down.

**Not in this cycle, and why.** Workstream A (multi-turn) is still the larger
build on the least-validated design in the repo. B1 (`x`/`x_local`) is still
gated on an M3 re-run that has not happened. C1 (auth) produces no number.
Top-*k* schema retrieval is **explicitly not started**: at nine tables
everything already fits the context, so selection is pure risk — and F-002-10
shows its stated safety property would be inert on the only real corpus anyway,
which is a reason to fix the corpus metadata first, not to build retrieval.

### The decision this plan needs from the owner

**Migration 008 departs from a recorded decision.** `migrations/007:31-36` says
*"NO CONSTRAINTS, DELIBERATELY … the DIRT IS THE POINT."* That is a `TRADEOFF`
under §4.2 with evidence predating the audit, and protocol says leave it and
cite it. I am asking rather than acting, with evidence 007 did not have: six of
the seven natural edges have **zero** orphans and would validate today without
touching a row. The dirt that motivated the decision lives on **one** edge
(`products → product_category_name_translation`, 13 orphans), and that edge is
handled explicitly rather than cleaned.

**Settled — owner, 2026-08-20.** Migration 008 goes in with the six clean edges;
the seventh is left **undeclared** rather than `NOT VALID`, so the dirty edge
stays dirty and stays visible. Scope confirmed as A + B as written. Both
recorded in the criteria below before the freeze, not after.

### A — Correct the record (close the audit's publishable findings)

**A1 (F-002-1).** The claim *"15 of ~74 categories have a genuine multi-way tie
at the category max price (e.g. `audio` has 6 products tied at 598.99)"* is
corrected everywhere it appears — `docs/metrics-report.md`,
`fixtures/golden/olist_hard.json`'s `oh_wf01` note, and
`scripts/gen_olist_hard_fixture.py`. The corrected text states the measured
figure (**4 of 71**), distinguishes tied *rows* from tied *distinct products*,
and states which of the two makes a top-1-per-partition question ambiguous. The
fixture is **regenerated by its generator**, never hand-edited, and every other
field is verified byte-identical to the committed version.

**A2 (F-002-2).** Two parts.
 (a) `docs/V2_STRATEGY.md` §4 B2 no longer credits the automated
 LIMIT-boundary check with finding `oh_wf01`. It states that the automated
 check found **zero** violations across the 13 questions it applies to, and
 that `oh_wf01` was a partition-internal tie found by the manual defect audit.
 (b) `gen_olist_hard_fixture.py` gains a **partition-internal tie check**: for
 every question whose ground truth selects one row per partition, the fixture
 is refused unless either no partition ties at rank 1 on its ranking metric, or
 the question is marked as stating its tie-break. **This guard must be
 demonstrated to FIRE on the shipped fixture** (`oh_wf01` ties in 4 categories;
 `oh_wf03`/`oh_wf04` tie in none) — a guard only ever seen to pass is not

**A3 (F-002-3).** A fixture↔corpus guard: any harness run against a fixture

refusal is **unconditional** — `--allow-nonshipped-flags` does not bypass it,
because a fixture/corpus mismatch is never the point of an experiment. Both
directions are asserted in a self-test that runs in CI, including a case
demonstrating refusal in `80d35c8`'s exact configuration
(`target_schema="public"` + an `olist` fixture).

### B — The join-graph experiment (F-002-10)

**B1. The asymmetry is recorded, unconditionally.** `docs/limitations.md` and
`docs/metrics-report.md` state that `public` declares 17 foreign-key edges over
10 tables while `olist` declares zero foreign keys and zero primary keys; that
`DirectSchemaRetriever._format_table` renders join information **only** from
declared foreign keys, so every real-data number this project has published was
measured with no join graph in the prompt; and that
`docs/schema_retrieval_decision.md`'s `SelectiveSchemaRetriever` safety
property — *"anything reachable by a foreign key … so join paths survive"* — is
inert on the only real corpus in this repo. **This criterion is met even if
B2–B5 do not run**: recording a confound is not conditional on measuring it.

**B2. `migrations/008_olist_join_graph.sql` adds metadata, never data.** It
adds primary-key/unique constraints to the referenced columns and the **six
zero-orphan** foreign-key edges. **No row is inserted, updated or deleted**, and
no value is cleaned. The seventh edge (`products →
product_category_name_translation`, **13** orphan products) is **left
undeclared** — owner decision, 2026-08-20, taken over the `NOT VALID`
alternative — and its absence is stated in the migration header, in
`docs/limitations.md` and at the point B5 publishes the result, with the orphan
count, so the one edge the model is still not shown is on record rather than
silently missing. The header cites `migrations/007:31-36`'s
deliberate no-constraints decision, states that this departs from it, and gives
the measured six-of-seven-zero-orphans evidence 007 did not have.

**B3. The corpus does not move, and the fingerprint does not move.** All 18
`olist.json` and all 16 `olist_hard.json` ground truths return **byte-identical
result sets** before and after 008 — verified programmatically, not by row
count. The A5 schema fingerprint is asserted unchanged, not assumed. Gates
green at the counts they hold today or better: parity 19/19 · seams 26/26 ·
pytest ≥32 · tenancy 12/12 · `shipped_flags --self-test` · `cycle --self-test`.
**M8 is re-run**, because this touches the data plane.

**B4. Paired measurement, one variable, both arms re-measured.**
`olist_hard.json` (n=16) and `olist.json` (n=18) are each run on local
`qwen2.5:7b` **before and after** 008, at the same flag state as `ecf5936`
(`normalization.enabled=false`, `target_schema=olist`, both recorded in
`flag_provenance`). The "before" arm is **re-measured rather than read from the
committed report**, so run-to-run variance is not silently absorbed into the
delta; if the re-measured before differs from the published `50.0%`/`88.9%`,
that difference is itself reported as a finding about instrument stability.

**B5. No threshold, in either direction, and both outcomes are published.** The
result is reported as measured in `metrics-report.md`, `V2_STRATEGY.md` and
`AGENT_HANDOFF.md`, with the caveat attached at each point of use. If FKs help,
the write-up states that the published real-data numbers were depressed by a
corpus-preparation asymmetry and that a declared join graph is a **deployment
precondition**, noting that many enterprise warehouses declare none. If FKs do
not help, the write-up states that a plausible confound under `50.0%` is
retired. Neither outcome is presented as the expected one.

**B6. The Gemini arm is optional and its omission is stated.** Gemini scores
`100.0%` on the hard fixture, so it has no headroom to improve and can only
show regression. If the free-tier budget allows, one post-008 Gemini run is
made; if it is not made, the write-up states that the experiment is
single-provider and that a regression on a stronger model would not have been
detected.
