### F-002-1 The corpus statistic behind the fixture's only published defect is wrong by 4×, and the example cited to illustrate it is a counter-example

- **Class:** 7 (ground truth / instrument evidence wrong) · 2 (a count measures
  something other than its name) · 6
- **Rank:** `PUBLISHABLE`
- **Bucket:** `DEFECT`

`docs/metrics-report.md` § "Hard-fixture results", the fixture's own
`oh_wf01.note`, and `ecf5936`'s commit message all state:

> 15 of ~74 real categories have a genuine multi-way tie at the category max
> price (e.g. `audio` has 6 products tied at 598.99)

Both halves are wrong, and wrong in the same direction: the count was taken
over **order_item rows**, not over **distinct products**. Rows of the same
product tied at the same price are not an ambiguity — the projected
`product_id` is identical either way. `audio`, the named illustration, is
exactly that case.

**Transcript**

```
$ # categories where >1 DISTINCT PRODUCT ties at the category max price
$ ... RANK() OVER (PARTITION BY category ORDER BY price DESC) ...
    HAVING count(DISTINCT product_id) > 1
[{"cat":"luggage_accessories","tied_products":2,"at_price":1197.9},
 {"cat":"furniture_mattress_and_upholstery","tied_products":2,"at_price":399.99},
 {"cat":"fashion_underwear_beach","tied_products":2,"at_price":192},
 {"cat":"fashion_childrens_clothes","tied_products":2,"at_price":110}]
                                                    -> 4 categories

$ # categories where >1 ROW ties at the category max price
    HAVING count(*) > 1
[{"categories_with_multirow_tie":15}]                -> 15, the published figure

$ # the cited example
SELECT oi.price, count(*) AS n_rows, count(DISTINCT oi.product_id) AS n_products
  ... WHERE t.product_category_name_english='audio' GROUP BY oi.price ORDER BY oi.price DESC LIMIT 3
[{"price":598.99,"n_rows":6,"n_products":1},
 {"price":590,   "n_rows":6,"n_products":1},
 {"price":586,   "n_rows":3,"n_products":1}]
                       -> 6 ROWS of ONE product. Zero ambiguity.

$ SELECT count(*) FROM olist.product_category_name_translation
[{"translation_rows":71,"distinct_english":71}]      -> 71, not "~74"
```

The correct statement is **4 of 71**, and `audio` is not one of them.

Context for the severity. Every *other* published corpus statistic in this
fixture reproduces byte-exactly — I checked all nine:

```
cs01 1161 ✓   cs02 329 ✓   cs03 23666 ✓   sellers 3095 ✓
j03 multi-review orders 555 ✓          orders 99441 ✓
distinct customer_id 99441 ✓           customer_unique_id 96096 ✓
wf04 multi-payment orders 2961 ✓       j04 multi-payment-type orders 2246 ✓
```

So this is not sloppiness across the board. It is one number, computed at the
wrong grain, and it happens to be the one number that justifies the fixture's
**sole published defect** — the row `olist_hard | 16 | 1 | 6.25%` in the
replication table `V2_STRATEGY.md` §6 puts forward as a paper claim.

**Does not establish:** this does **not** invalidate the 6.25% defect rate or
the `oh_wf01` fix. A genuine multi-product tie *does* exist in 4 categories,
so the wording fix was warranted and the defect is real — its stated magnitude
is not. Nor does it touch the ground truth: I re-executed all 16 references
and confirmed independently that none of the 5 categories in `oh_wf01`'s
shipped output is among the 4 genuinely-tied ones, so the shipped answer was
never wrong. It says nothing about `olist.json`'s 18 references, which audit
001 verified line by line and I did not re-verify.
