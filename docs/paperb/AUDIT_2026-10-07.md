learning steps. None of them is knowledge a reader can use beyond StateSync, so they do not belong in the
abstract or the contributions.

## 2. Every experiment, checked

Verdicts: **keep** (sound, reportable), **fix analysis** (the data are fine, the comparison is not),
**rerun** (needs new data to be credible), **drop** (no contribution, or confounded beyond repair).

### Cross-cutting

- **Graders.** Document answers and the page questions both come from Gemini 3.8 Flash. The 30-answer check
  is a second model, not a human (reported as such). The routing-label and hybrid spot checks were also
  model-filled, so they cannot be called human validation either.
- **Authored data.** The routing set's sql/hybrid/neither questions and the hybrid set are ours (templates and
  hand-written), labelled by our own rules.
- **Provider disclosure.** Several models were reached through routers that do not name their upstream; the
  served model is recorded per call.

## 3. What is actually new

Stripped of the product history, the project has evidence for one coherent question that the literature has
not answered: **can a small hosted decision model that returns typed probabilities replace the LLM calls in a
database-and-documents assistant that only decide something?**

1. Verifying SQL answers: zero-shot Jev detects wrong answers close to an LLM judge and a fine-tuned verifier
   (S4), and as a cheap trigger it may cut fallback cost (S5, exploratory).
2. Routing: as a single joint choice it is close to an LLM router with no LLM call; posed as independent
   yes/no questions it cannot represent "both" (D4). This is a general point about decomposing a joint
   decision into marginal probabilities.
3. Re-ranking: zero-shot, it matches a GPU cross-encoder on held-out generated questions and beats it on
   FinanceBench's real questions (D8, corrected).
4. Detect-then-fallback for SQL: a cheap trigger plus a multi-model pool recovers most of what always
   selecting from the pool recovers, at about half the cost (S2, reframed).
5. Negative results with reasons: trained verifiers rank no better than voting after correction (H1); a
   verified label needs far more calibration data than a setup step has (S7, as a sample-size statement);
   Jev cannot judge prose against evidence (D10).
